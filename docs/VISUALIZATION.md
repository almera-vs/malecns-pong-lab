# Visualization

The overview is one persistent 2D x/z anatomical projection. Every recorded soma is positioned from the normalized MaleCNS 8 nm coordinate transform. Neurons without recorded coordinates remain in the neural graph but receive no soma marker. The loaded label reports both counts. Colors indicate the loader’s explicit optic, central, VNC, motor/descending, or other section classification. Population checkboxes hide/show elements without changing neural state, and the cached anatomy remains visible even when no spikes are present.

The latest completed batch is painted at most 30 times per second. The overlay supports at most 2,048 activity markers, but the current worker already limits the supplied IDs to 512 per batch. At most 384 traversed connections appear as transient lines. These are display limits: internal spike counts and plasticity retain full simulated activity. Stored edges with unresolved source signs do not transmit current, independently of display sampling. When batches arrive faster than painting, the newest overlay replaces the pending one; batch records and sampled raster rows are retained.

Selecting a neuron shows body ID, annotated type, section, normalized position, recent spike ticks, batch firing rate, membrane/activity trace, and incoming/outgoing connection counts. The brain card also includes a procedural WebGL fruit fly facing the viewer at 45 degrees, with a banded abdomen, faceted red eyes, antennae, bristles, translucent veined wings, and six segmented legs. Foreleg flexion follows live embodiment angles one-to-one across the entire ±π/2 range with fixed segment lengths, without display amplification or a clipping plateau. Small joint markers show muscle activation. The other four legs hold a resting pose; no idle gait is presented as neural output. This is an illustrative model, not a measured anatomical reconstruction.

The fly uses the application's existing animation loop, interpolates incoming poses, and paints at most 30 times per second. Static body and wing geometry are uploaded once. Its canvas has a CSS-controlled 320px height; resizing updates only the pixel backing store, avoiding layout feedback. The visualization sends no inputs to the brain or paddle mechanics and freezes with the Pause control.

The raster bins every supplied display spike ID by horizontal pixel column and annotated section. Because the worker sends a sample, this is not a complete population spike train. Horizontal position is derived from graph index, not anatomical distance. Each batch scrolls the bitmap by three pixels; overlapping cells in a pixel/section are collapsed. Numerical spike-count cards retain full totals. The trace and labels refresh at most every 150 ms, with event/final-paused updates. The game contact counter has a separate left-hit-only limitation documented in the experiment protocol.

The visible Pong court animates at the browser's refresh cadence by interpolating committed states, independently of worker throughput. It never extrapolates a future state or supplies interpolated pixels to the brain. The binocular preview paints at most 30 times per second; the brain still receives an exact area-averaged frame on every completed batch. The preview shows two overlapping court crops plus 64 luminance channels. The interface audit reports which annotated photoreceptors receive those channels and whether channel assignment used explicit retinotopy or the soma-coordinate proxy. These image bins are not ommatidia or a validated fly-eye optical model.

Neural time is simulation time; wall time measures the completed batch. The displayed neural-time/wall-time ratio is a performance diagnostic, not a biological timescale claim. A 60 FPS presentation is not a 60 Hz neural update rate or a claim of real-time full-brain simulation. The timing element's `data-profile` attribute exposes worker, raster, overlay, retina, batch, and animation measurements for browser diagnostics.
